Flame rectification

How a boiler uses the flame itself as part of an electrical circuit to prove it is alight.

Why dust or condensation can't fake a flame
Dirt or moisture on the probe might leak a.c. to earth, but it conducts equally both ways — so it produces no d.c. The control box ignores it. Only a real flame rectifies.
Where it's used
Commonly fitted to boilers with full-sequence automatic burner control. Advantages: instant response and immediate shut-down on flame failure.
Polarity & earthing
Highly reliable, but correct electrical polarity and a good earth are critical. Reversed polarity or a poor earth can stop the flame being sensed, causing lockout.
Testing & faults
Measure the flame current with a meter that reads micro-amps (µA), connected in series in the flame circuit at the point the manufacturer specifies, and compare it with the manufacturer's figure. If the device is faulty, replace the unit.
