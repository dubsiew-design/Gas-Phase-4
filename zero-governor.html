How a zero governor maintains the air-to-gas ratio

The relationship between fan speed, Venturi pressure, gas regulation and efficient combustion. Also called an air-to-gas ratio valve, air/gas valve or 1:1 gas valve.

This animation is for educational purposes only. Gas appliances must be tested, adjusted and serviced only by qualified personnel, following the appliance manufacturer's instructions. Never adjust a gas valve unless the manufacturer specifically permits it. Combustion adjustments must be verified with an approved combustion analyser.
Two common arrangements
Negative pressure: the fan is after the valve and pulls air through it. Positive pressure: the fan is before the valve and pushes air through it. The principle is the same: a change in air flow creates a pressure change that controls the gas regulator. Use the tabs to compare.
What it does — and doesn't do
The valve responds to the pressure created by the air flow. It does not measure oxygen or combustion quality, so the mixture must always be checked with an approved combustion analyser.
Offset & throttle
Some zero governors have an offset adjustment and a throttle adjustment. The correct method depends entirely on the appliance and valve manufacturer. Never adjust by guesswork or by looking at the flame. Some manufacturers specify very small steps (such as a maximum quarter-turn between tests) — follow this only where their instructions say so.
Faults (from the course notes)
Incorrect adjustment of the offset · incorrect adjustment of the throttle · failed system component.
