Overheat thermostats

The high-limit stat that cuts the burner if the boiler water gets too hot — two common types.

1. Bi-metallic disc (most common)
A small, button-sized switch clipped directly onto a hot copper pipe or the heat exchanger.
  • Two bonded metals: a curved disc of two different metals (like brass and steel) bonded together.
  • Differential expansion: the metals expand at different rates, so the disc builds up tension as the boiler warms up.
  • Snap action: at the factory-set limit (typically about 95–105°C) the disc snaps from concave to convex, pushing open the contacts and cutting power to the gas valve.
  • Resetting: manual-reset stats hold the contacts open until you press the reset button; auto-reset stats snap back once cool.
2. Capillary bulb & bellows (older boilers)
Uses fluid expansion rather than flexing metal.
  • Sensing bulb: a small copper bulb filled with a heat-sensitive liquid, in a pocket in the heat exchanger.
  • Capillary tube: a thin copper tube connects the bulb to a flexible metal bellows in the control box.
  • Pressure trip: as the water overheats the liquid expands, forcing pressure into the bellows. The bellows expands like an accordion and trips a spring-loaded microswitch, breaking the circuit to the burner.
